CobbAI Clinical Validation Report

This page documents the methodology and preliminary results of the clinical validation study comparing CobbAI automated Cobb angle measurements against expert radiologist measurements.

📋 Study Overview

CobbAI uses a convolutional neural network approach to automatically measure the Cobb angle from standing PA spine X-rays. This validation study follows the methodology of Caesarendra et al. (Diagnostics 2022) and Maeda et al. (Scientific Reports 2023).

Methodology reference:
  • Measurement standard: Cobb (1948) — endplate lines of most-tilted end vertebrae
  • 68-landmark detection: AASCE MICCAI 2019 — 17 vertebrae (T1–L5), 4 corners each
  • End vertebra selection: Caesarendra et al. 2022 — two-phase slope analysis
  • Inclination filter: Maeda et al. 2023 — discard if adjacent slope diff <5°
⚠️ Important Notice: The metrics below marked as Pending require prospective data collection from clinical partners. CobbAI is currently in the research and development phase. Results should not be used for standalone clinical diagnosis.

🔬 Validation Methodology

STUDY DESIGN

  • Retrospective cross-sectional study using anonymised standing PA spine X-rays
  • Expert ground truth: average of two independent measurements by board-certified radiologists
  • Observers: minimum 2 observers (1 spine specialist, 1 general radiologist)
  • Intraobserver reproducibility: second measurement after ≥3 weeks washout
  • AI measurement: fully automated, no human input

INCLUSION CRITERIA

  • Diagnosis of adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) confirmed by a clinician
  • Standing full-spine PA or AP radiograph (T1 to L5/sacrum visible)
  • Cobb angle ≥10° on any curve
  • Age 10–18 years (or adult AIS if explicitly noted)

EXCLUSION CRITERIA

  • Neuromuscular or congenital scoliosis
  • Previous spinal surgery
  • Non-standing radiographs (supine, bending films)
  • Radiographs with significant rotation artefact or inadequate field of view

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS

  • ICC (2,1) — Two-way random-effects model for interobserver reliability (AI vs expert)
  • MAE — Mean absolute error between AI and expert average
  • RMSE — Root mean square error
  • Bland-Altman plot — Limits of agreement (mean ± 1.96 SD)
  • Pearson correlation — Linear correlation coefficient (r)
  • Stratified analysis by: angle magnitude (<20°, 20–40°, >40°), curve location, observer experience

ICC thresholds: <0.70 poor | 0.70–0.79 fair | 0.80–0.89 good | >0.90 excellent (Koo & Li, 2016).

The Bland-Altman plot shows the agreement between AI and expert measurements. Points should cluster within the 95% limits of agreement (mean ± 1.96 SD). Systematic bias is indicated if the mean difference deviates significantly from zero.

⚠️ Current Limitations

  • AI model has not yet been prospectively validated on a clinically representative cohort
  • Optimal performance on standing PA/AP full-spine radiographs only — not validated on supine, bending, or brace films
  • Image quality significantly affects accuracy — poor or rotated films should be re-acquired if possible
  • Currently measures the primary and secondary curves only; triple or complex patterns may be less accurate
  • Not validated for non-AIS scoliosis (neuromuscular, congenital, degenerative)
  • AI measurement should be verified by a qualified clinician before clinical decision-making

⚖️ Ethics & Data Privacy

All X-ray images processed by CobbAI are transmitted securely to the Google Gemini API for analysis only. No images are stored permanently on CobbAI servers. Each session's data is deleted upon completion.

Validation data collection requires IRB/ethics committee approval from the participating institution. Patient consent and data anonymization must comply with applicable regulations (GDPR, KVKK, HIPAA as appropriate).

⚕ Clinical Disclaimer: CobbAI is a decision-support tool for qualified physicians and physiotherapists. It is not intended to replace clinical judgment or act as a standalone diagnostic device. All measurements must be interpreted in the context of the full clinical presentation by a licensed clinician.
